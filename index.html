How It Works

A clinical workflow, extended into the home.

Step 1

The therapist builds the plan

A licensed physical therapist assigns a personalized exercise program directly from their existing evaluation. It is the same plan they would otherwise hand to a patient on paper.

Step 2

Axion guides the patient at home

Using the camera on a phone or tablet, Axion tracks movement, counts repetitions, and flags form that falls outside the prescribed range.

Step 3

The therapist reviews progress

Adherence, repetition counts, and form data flow back to the clinician dashboard, so therapists can adjust the plan or step in early.

Technology

Built as clinical infrastructure, not a fitness feature.

Every layer of Axion is designed to a clinical standard of accuracy, privacy, and reliability, from movement capture to the data therapists use to make decisions.

Perception

Computer vision

Tracking built on a standard camera identifies joint position and body pose in real time, processed on the device itself where possible.

Analysis

Motion analysis

Movement trajectories are compared against the prescribed exercise pattern to assess range, tempo, and form.

Personalization

Adaptive rehabilitation algorithms

Difficulty, pacing, and feedback adjust session to session based on the patient's measured performance.

Engagement

Guided assistance

Structured prompts and encouragement keep repetitive exercise sessions easier to stay consistent with.

Infrastructure

Secure health data handling

Patient data is encrypted in transit and at rest, with access permissions matched to each clinical role.

Clinical Fit

Designed with input from orthopedic surgeons and licensed physical therapists throughout development.

Research

Built on a growing body of published research.

Axion's approach draws on an active, independent research area studying computer vision and AI for physical rehabilitation. These studies are not evaluations of Axion itself. They are cited here to show the evidence base behind the underlying approach.

JMIR mHealth and uHealth, 2025

Randomized controlled trial of a computer vision exercise application for knee osteoarthritis

Patients using a computer vision based exercise application showed improved physical function and self efficacy compared with a control group receiving standard care.

View study

Scientific Reports, 2025

A real time action scoring system for movement analysis and feedback in physical therapy

Researchers describe a human pose estimation system that gives real time feedback on exercise movement, and note its potential to support patient adherence to prescribed rehabilitation exercises.

View study

PMC narrative review, 2025

AI driven virtual assistants in home rehabilitation

A review of thirty one studies found that AI driven virtual platforms combining cameras or wearable sensors with real time feedback were associated with improved exercise adherence and fewer in person visits.

About Axion

Closing the gap between clinical rehabilitation and recovery at home.

Axion began after extensive time inside physical therapy clinics, orthopedic research settings, and rehabilitation programs, working alongside physical therapists, orthopedic surgeons, rehabilitation researchers, and the patients recovering under their care.

Across every setting, the same pattern kept showing up. Patients received a carefully designed exercise program, then struggled to keep up with it once they left the clinic. This was not from a lack of effort. It came from a lack of structure, feedback, and accountability once no one was watching.

Axion was built to close that gap: giving patients a guided way to do their exercises correctly at home, and giving therapists the visibility to support them through the entire course of recovery, not just the minutes spent in the clinic.
